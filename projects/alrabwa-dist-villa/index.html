Overview

A private villa on a 675 m² plot in Riyadh, designed around three living zones with carefully controlled circulation. The brief calls for privacy at ground level, selected connections above, and a double-height living space that maintains a visual relationship with the first floor.

Concept & process

The organizing idea is a changing relationship between zones across the building’s levels. Zones 1 and 2 remain separate on the ground floor, while connections on the first floor and roof allow them to function together when required. Zone 2’s double-height space adds a vertical connection within this arrangement.

Ground-floor, first-floor and roof plans are read together with building sections and BIM cutaways. They document the circulation cores, open-to-below spaces and upper-level connections. The façade render set presents the exterior through daytime and evening views, showing light stone surfaces, recessed glazing, timber accents and planted terraces.

Challenges & achievements

Design challenge

Accommodate three distinct living zones with limited circulation between them while maintaining one villa rather than three fully walled-off subdivisions.

Design achievement

Developed a level-by-level zoning strategy that balances household privacy with selected internal connections, responding to the client’s needs and the regulatory constraint described in the brief.

Design challenge

Keep Zones 1 and 2 separate on the ground floor while allowing them to connect on the first floor and roof level.

Design achievement

Concentrated their shared connections on the upper levels, preserving independent ground-floor circulation.

Design challenge

Give Zone 2 a double-height ground-floor space while retaining connections to the first floor.

Design achievement

Used a double-height living space and overlooking upper-level circulation to create visual continuity without opening every ground-floor zone to one another.
