Overview

One family commission on an 800 m² plot divided into two neighboring homes: the father’s residence on the left and the son’s residence on the right. Each household has an independent layout, while the two buildings are presented as a coordinated residential project.

Concept & process

The design balances family proximity with household independence. Separate entrances, reception areas, parking and vertical circulation allow the two homes to operate individually. Stepped volumes and setback roof annexes connect the two residences; the revised exterior render set presents light stone surfaces, darker panels and recessed glazing.

The father’s home organizes family living and guest reception around its own circulation core, with bedrooms above and an outdoor roof terrace. The son’s corner home includes a basement with living, dining and multipurpose spaces, open-to-above lightwells, upper-level bedrooms and roof terraces. Plans, sections and BIM cutaways trace how these different programs fit within the divided site.

Challenges & achievements

Design challenge

Accommodating two generations on one divided plot while preserving each household’s privacy and independence.

Design achievement

Two distinct homes within one family project, each with its own entrances, parking and vertical circulation.

Design challenge

Bringing light and outdoor connections into a dense, multi-level arrangement, including the son’s basement.

Design achievement

Courtyards, open-to-above/below spaces and roof terraces that connect the interior layouts to outdoor space.

Design challenge

Organizing guest reception, family living, bedrooms and service spaces around separate circulation needs.

Design achievement

Dedicated reception and family zones, with lifts and stairs linking the residential levels; the father’s first-floor study also includes an alternative bedroom arrangement.
